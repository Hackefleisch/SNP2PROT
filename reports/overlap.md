# Cross-source overlap — the label noise floor

Regenerated by `scripts/make_overlap_report.py`. Do not hand-edit.

**47 domains are measured by more than one source**, giving 49 source pairs of the same protein against the same 8-mer set. Two labs, one sequence: the rate at which they disagree is a direct measurement of the label noise floor, and nothing else in the corpus provides it.

## The headline

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| hard-label agreement (pooled) | 99.981% (300 of 1,572,985 calls differ) |
| **positive-call Jaccard (median)** | **0.458** |
| positive-call Jaccard (min / max) | 0.000 / 0.976 |
| positives agreed on (pooled) | 2,863 of 5,816 (49.2%) |
| E-score rank correlation (median) | 0.608 |
| E-score rank correlation (min) | 0.068 |

**Read the second row, not the first.** Positives are a fraction of a percent of every 8-mer table, so two labs agreeing that the other 99.6% does not bind pins the pooled agreement near 100% however badly they agree about binding. On the calls the dataset exists to make, the median replicate pair agrees on 46% of the 8-mers either of them called positive.

## Which record survives the merge

One record per domain reaches `data/processed/`: **the one with more positives**, unless one candidate belongs to a variant series, in which case the series wins (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, 2026-08-18). A PBM fails by missing binding, not by inventing it, so the deeper measurement is the more informative one — and leaving both would hand a model identical input with two different labels, inside one cluster, where no split can separate them.

**48 of 95 records are dropped at merge**, 1,579,008 rows. Nothing is deleted: `data/interim/` keeps every measurement, which is what this report is computed from.
